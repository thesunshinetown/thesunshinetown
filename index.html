Est. 1761

A mill town shaped by the Sugar River

Granted by colonial governor Benning Wentworth and first settled by families from Killingworth, Connecticut, Newport grew from a frontier outpost into a center of New Hampshire's woolen mill industry.

Newport was chartered in 1753 and originally named "Grenville," after a British Prime Minister. War with the French and their Native allies delayed settlement, and it wasn't until the mid-1760s that the first permanent families arrived from Connecticut, clearing land along what would become the town center.

The Sugar River and its branches gave Newport its industrial edge. Mill owners across the region built a dam to keep water power flowing even through drought, and by the mid-1800s more than a hundred water wheels turned along the river, driving woolen mills, tanneries, and the Sibley Scythe Company — whose tools were used during construction of the Panama Canal.

Today, landmarks like the 1823 South Congregational Church, the 1886 Newport Opera House, and the Richards Free Library still anchor a town that has reinvented itself more than once — from farms, to mills, to shoe shops, to the small-business and arts community it is now.

For the site owner

Keeping this page current

This page reads two small files — events.json and news.json — at load time. Editing those files is all it takes to update the site; no code changes needed.

Manual updates (simplest): open events.json or news.json in any text editor, add an entry following the existing format, and re-upload to your host. Takes a couple of minutes a day.

Semi-automated: put the data in a Google Sheet instead, publish it as JSON (or use a free tool like Sheet.best), and point the fetch calls at that URL. You update a spreadsheet; the page reflects it within seconds.

Fully automated: run a small script on a daily schedule (a cron job, or a free scheduled job on Render/GitHub Actions) that pulls headlines from local RSS feeds — e.g. the Eagle Times, NH Union Leader's Sullivan County coverage, the Town of Newport's official announcements — and rewrites news.json automatically. A starter Python script for this is included with the site files.

This site is currently a static page. For full automation you'll need real hosting (not just a flat file) — see the README included with these files for specific options and rough costs.
